A Tribute

Nikola Tesla

Inventor of the Modern World

1856 – 1943
Legacy

Nikola Tesla was a Serbian-American inventor, electrical engineer, and futurist whose discoveries form the invisible backbone of modern civilization — from the electricity in your walls to the radio waves carrying your voice.

Born on the stroke of midnight during a lightning storm in Smiljan, Serbia, Tesla seemed destined for a life entwined with electricity. He arrived in America in 1884 with four cents in his pocket, a letter of recommendation, and an extraordinary mind that would reshape the world.

A Life in Milestones

  • 1856Born in Smiljan, Serbia on July 10th.
  • 1882Conceived the rotating magnetic field principle while walking in a park in Budapest.
  • 1884Emigrated to the United States and began working for Thomas Edison.
  • 1887Developed the AC induction motor, solving a fundamental problem in electrical engineering.
  • 1891Invented the Tesla Coil, a resonant transformer circuit still used in radio technology today.
  • 1893Lit the World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago with alternating current — a watershed moment for AC power.
  • 1895Designed the first AC hydroelectric power plant at Niagara Falls with Westinghouse.
  • 1898Demonstrated the world's first radio-controlled boat at Madison Square Garden.
  • 1899Conducted high-voltage experiments at Colorado Springs, transmitting electricity wirelessly.
  • 1943Died alone in Room 3327 of the New Yorker Hotel. The Supreme Court later credited him as the inventor of radio.

The present is theirs; the future, for which I really worked, is mine.

— Nikola Tesla

Tesla's rivalry with Edison over alternating versus direct current — the so-called "War of Currents" — is one of history's great intellectual battles. Tesla's AC system, championed by George Westinghouse, ultimately prevailed, powering homes and industries across the world in a form we still use today.

Despite his immense contributions, Tesla died in obscurity and poverty. History has since corrected this injustice. Today, the SI unit of magnetic flux density bears his name, and the world's most celebrated electric car company carries his legacy into the future.
